### Memory systems
* ![](/attachments/transformative-tools-for-thought-image.png)Here we see the first major benefit of the mnemonic medium, unlike most other things in life here we get exponential rather than diminishing return for each review session when it comes to memory capacity
* One of the ideas motivating *Quantum Country* is that memory systems aren’t just useful for simple declarative knowledge, such as vocabulary words and lists of capitals. In fact, memory systems can be extraordinarily helpful for mastering abstract, conceptual knowledge, the kind of knowledge required to learn subjects such as quantum mechanics and quantum computing. This is achieved in part through many detailed strategies for constructing cards capable of encoding this kind of understanding. But, more importantly, it’s possible because of the way the mnemonic medium embeds spaced repetition inside a narrative. That narrative embedding makes it possible for context and understanding to build in ways difficult in other memory systems.
  * Memory ststems have historically most commonly been used for memorization of simple declarative facts. Using these to remember abstract, conceptual knowledge is also possible, however it requires a high amount of skill - resulting in the fact that they are used in this way by only a handful of people.
* Answering the question of “how to write good cards?” Requires thinking about your theory of knowledge and how to represent it, and your theory of learning
* In fact, there are ideas about memory very different from spaced repetition, but of comparable power. One such idea is *elaborative encoding*. Roughly speaking, this is the idea that the richer the associations we have to a concept, the better we will remember it. As a consequence, we can improve our memory by enriching that network of associations
* spaced repetition is generally more broad than other, narrower specialized mnemonic techniques 
* A common objection raised in opposition to practices focused on honing your memory is “what is the use of memory anyway”, I prefer to focus more on conceptual knowledge and creativity than rote memorization. However, until one manages to remember the basics thoroughly he can have trouble understanding complex subjects as they are built of many building blocks that he is unaware of. 
* A key fact to remember is that memory systems do not place restrictions on what they help you remember, hence it is up to you to make sure you memorize important facts and not orphaned knowledge/ subjects you don’t actually care for. The fundamental question “what will be beneaivial to memorize”
* The authors propose the insight-through-making paradigm, one in which you conduct deep research and thoroughly understand and ponder a subject until a fascinating thought arises, but once that happens you must implement it from start to finish to patch up the implementation details and shore up the limitations inherent in your initial conception of the problem.
